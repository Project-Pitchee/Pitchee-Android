# Low Timbre Score: Stop Forcing the Voice

A low timbre score means the recording does not sound feminine enough overall.

It indicates that the current resonance and intonation do not give the system enough feminine vocal features.

Pitch can be high while the timbre score remains low. You can speak at 200 Hz and still sound thick, muffled, pressed, or obviously put on.

## Common mistakes

### Pushing pitch only

Pitch is only one part of timbre. If pitch rises while the voice remains thick, muffled, or artificial, the timbre score will not improve.

### Clamping continuously

Clamping the voice may brighten short phrases immediately, but the voice easily becomes tighter and naturalness falls with it.

### Using breathy voice continuously

Breathy voice is not the same as feminine voice. Too much air weakens the sound and may prevent reliable pitch detection.

## Practice

### 1. Record a baseline

Read in your normal voice:

> Adlerian psychology divides interpersonal problems into “my tasks” and “other people’s tasks.”

Do not press the voice down deliberately or clamp upward at the start. Keep this recording as the baseline.

### 2. Release the mouth and tongue

Gently move the jaw, tongue, and lips. At a comfortable volume, hum `m` and then move into `ma` and `me`.

The voice only needs to come out smoothly. Stop if you feel pain, squeezing, or obvious strain.

### 3. Focus the sound

Read the passage again, this time focusing on clear articulation. Do not trap the sound at the back of the mouth.

Do not raise pitch, increase volume, and switch to breathy voice at the same time. Change one variable at a time.

### 4. Test with a new sentence

> Stoicism divides things into two categories: what is within your control and what is not.

If only the practice passage improves and a new sentence falls apart, the change has not been learned yet.

## Daily practice

Practice for 10 to 15 minutes a day. Rotate three kinds of material:

1. One prepared sentence.
2. A 20-second reading passage.
3. One improvised answer.

Then record at least 5 seconds in Pitchee and check the change in timbre score. The timbre model was trained on connected speech, so speak normally and do not record a sustained vowel.

## When to stop

Stop if you feel pain, persistent hoarseness, obvious strain, or a voice that takes a long time to recover after speaking.
