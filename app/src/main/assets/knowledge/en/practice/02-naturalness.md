# Low Naturalness: Return the Voice to Normal Speech

Low naturalness means the recording does not sound like normal speech.

The voice may be too forceful, too artificial, or affected by poor recording conditions.

Low naturalness does not mean the voice is damaged. It means the delivery has moved away from everyday speech.

## Stop adding techniques

When naturalness is low, many people respond by learning more bright voice, sweet voice, breathy voice, and intonation techniques. This usually makes the problem worse.

The missing element is not another technique. The voice needs to become conversational again.

## Three common causes

### 1. Performing every line

Every word is forceful, and every sentence ending rises or falls in the same way. It sounds like an advertisement, a character performance, or deliberately cute speech.

### 2. Holding the voice in place

To maintain a high pitch or a particular timbre, the throat, tongue, and breathing remain tense. The sound may have the target pitch and still sound rigid.

### 3. Distorted recording

The microphone may be too close, the input level too high, or the recording affected by background noise, reverb, or noise reduction.

## Practice

### 1. Give the sentence a listener

Read a passage:

> I’m free tomorrow morning. Let’s go have dim sum.

Imagine the other person is standing in front of you.

### 2. Relax fully

Produce the lowest vocal fry you can, ideally with audible creaking. Gradually raise the pitch until you return to your normal speaking pitch.

### 3. Keep recordings short

Record for 10 to 20 seconds each time. Long repetitions introduce fatigue, and later recordings become unsuitable for comparison.

## Do not use quiet voice as a substitute for natural voice

A natural voice can be clear, supported, and at normal volume.

Do not turn the voice into a whisper, breathy sound, or progressively smaller sound just to reduce effort.
